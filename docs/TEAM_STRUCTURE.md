# Core Project Team Structure

## Overview

This document defines the core team structure for the Notion Task Tracker Agent project. The team composition has been carefully designed to balance technical expertise, product vision, and quality assurance within a lean and efficient structure.

## Team Composition

The project operates with a **4-5 person core team**, ensuring focused collaboration while maintaining comprehensive coverage of all critical project functions.

### Visual Hierarchy

```
Project Team (4-5 people)
├── Product Manager (1)
├── Technical Lead (1)
├── Backend/CLI Developer (2)
└── Quality Assurance (1)
```

### Team Size Rationale

The 4-5 person team structure has been selected for the following reasons:

1. **Optimal Communication**: Small enough to maintain direct, efficient communication without coordination overhead
2. **Balanced Skillsets**: Provides adequate coverage across product management, technical leadership, development, and quality assurance
3. **Resource Efficiency**: Lean structure suitable for MVP development while ensuring quality deliverables
4. **Clear Accountability**: Each role has defined ownership areas, preventing duplication of effort
5. **Scalability Foundation**: Structure can scale appropriately post-MVP based on project needs

## Role Definitions

### 6.2.1 Product Manager (1 person)

The Product Manager serves as the primary stakeholder liaison and business owner of the project.

#### Responsibilities
- Define and prioritize feature roadmap
- Gather user requirements and feedback
- Coordinate between stakeholders and development team
- Manage project timeline and milestones
- Ensure business requirements are met

#### MVP Deliverables

- Feature prioritization and scope
- User experience requirements
- Release timing and milestones

## Reporting Structure

### Decision-Making Hierarchy

The team operates with a flat structure but recognizes two key decision-makers:

1. **Product Manager**: Final authority on product scope, features, and user requirements
2. **Technical Lead**: Final authority on technical architecture, implementation approach, and technical standards

### Cross-Functional Collaboration

While the hierarchy establishes decision-making authority, the team operates collaboratively:

- **Daily Standups**: All team members participate in daily sync meetings
- **Sprint Planning**: Product Manager and Technical Lead co-lead planning sessions
- **Code Reviews**: Technical Lead reviews all code; Backend Developers peer-review each other
- **Testing Cycles**: QA Engineer coordinates with all developers for testing activities

## Communication Channels

### Internal Team Communication
- **Daily Standups**: 15-minute sync every morning
- **Sprint Planning**: Bi-weekly 2-hour planning sessions
- **Retrospectives**: End of sprint review and improvement sessions
- **Ad-hoc Discussions**: Slack/Teams channels for real-time communication

### Stakeholder Communication
- **Weekly Status Updates**: Led by Product Manager
- **Demo Sessions**: End of sprint demonstrations
- **Monthly Reviews**: Progress review with key stakeholders

## Success Metrics

The team structure will be evaluated based on:

1. **Delivery Velocity**: Ability to meet sprint commitments and milestone targets
2. **Quality Metrics**: Bug rates, test coverage, performance benchmarks
3. **Team Satisfaction**: Regular pulse checks on team collaboration and workload
4. **Stakeholder Feedback**: Product Manager gathers feedback on team responsiveness

---

## Future Scalability

As the project grows beyond MVP, the team structure may evolve:

### Potential Growth Areas
- **Additional Backend Developers**: As feature complexity increases
- **DevOps Engineer**: For CI/CD and infrastructure automation
- **UX/UI Designer**: If GUI components are added
- **Developer Advocate**: For community engagement and support

### Scaling Principles
- Maintain small, focused teams (5-7 people max per team)
- Add roles based on demonstrated bottlenecks, not speculation
- Preserve direct communication channels as team grows
- Maintain 1:1 ratio between PM and Tech Lead as teams multiply
